# Ante Quem — design spec

**Date:** 2026-09-09
**Status:** approved, not yet implemented
**Repo:** standalone. Nothing in this project belongs to, or may be committed to, the `rolls` repo.

## What it is

Two browser games that teach you to read a medieval manuscript by eye, built entirely on the
Bodleian's open TEI catalogue. Every round ends with a link back to the Bodleian's own record.

- **Ante Quem** — fast, comparative, daily, streak-based. Place manuscripts in time.
- **Look Closer** — slow, solitary, exploratory. Interrogate one page and commit to a reading.

They share one extraction, one data file, one visual language, and one collection layer.

## Who it is for

Medievalists, who will go straight to hard mode and argue with the scoring; and the interested
public, who need no prior knowledge to play round one. The design requirement is that both are
served by the same build — the difficulty is a dial, not a separate product.

## Source data

`github.com/bodleian/medieval-mss` @ `master` — 11,123 TEI `msDesc` records, 110 MB of XML,
plus three authority files: `persons.xml` (13.8 MB), `works.xml` (8.7 MB), `places.xml` (1.7 MB).

Fetch the working tree, not the history:

    curl -sSL https://codeload.github.com/bodleian/medieval-mss/tar.gz/refs/heads/master

21 MB compressed, 160 MB extracted.

**Licence — resolve before public launch.** The repository has no LICENSE file. Image
attribution is unambiguous (CC BY-NC 4.0, credit line supplied in each IIIF manifest's
`attribution` field, and it must be displayed). The catalogue *text* has no stated licence.
Ask the Bodleian rather than assuming.

## Verified data facts

All figures below were counted from the real corpus on 2026-09-09, not estimated.

| Property | Whole corpus | The 2,260 playable records |
|---|---|---|
| Records | 11,123 | 2,260 |
| Machine-readable `origDate` range | 9,477 (85%) | 100% |
| `origPlace` resolving to coordinates | 7,466 (67%) | 100% |
| Digital Bodleian images | 3,425 (31%) | 100% |
| Cataloguer-assigned **subject** (via work keys) | — | **2,226 (98%)** |
| `handNote` — description of the script | 22% | **828 (37%)** |
| `layoutDesc` — columns and ruling | 29% | **1,189 (53%)** |
| `incipit` — transcribed opening words | 14% | **710 (31%)** |
| `decoNote` | 47% | 86% |
| Named former owners | 53% | 72% |

"Playable" means: has a `notBefore`/`notAfter` date range, an origin place with coordinates,
and at least one Digital Bodleian image.

Other measured properties that drive the design:

- **Median date-range span is 25 years.** Tight enough to compare honestly.
- **82% of random record pairs have non-overlapping date ranges** — an unambiguous "which is older".
- **Date gaps available:** 25% of pairs ≥300 years apart, 56% ≥100y, 77% ≥25y. Enough for a
  difficulty ladder at every rung.
- **The set clumps in the 15th century** — 918 of 2,260 (41%). Decks must be stratified or the
  timeline board fills in one place.
- **2,056 of 2,260 sit in a date+place bucket with 6+ other illustrated manuscripts**, so the
  look-alike strip almost always fires.

### Origin places are countries, not points

| Place | Count | Type |
|---|---|---|
| England | 785 (35%) | country |
| Italia | 469 (21%) | country |
| Deutschland | 351 (16%) | country |
| France | 276 (12%) | country |
| Al Bahnasā (Oxyrhynchus) | 82 (4%) | settlement |

46 distinct places; the top five are 87% of the set, four of them whole countries. **Place is
therefore a five-way multiple choice, never a map click.** Distance-in-kilometres against a
France-sized polygon is fake precision and was cut from the design for that reason.

### Subject comes from the authority file, not from us

`works.xml` carries a controlled vocabulary of ~25 cataloguer-assigned subjects, referenced as
`<term ref="#subject_liturgy"/>` on each work record. Resolving `msItem/title/@key` into
`works.xml` reaches a subject for **98%** of playable records.

Distribution: liturgy 22%, theology 13%, bible 13%, history 7%, literature 6%,
classical_literature 6%, sermons 6%, science 4%, christian_literature 3%, documents 3%,
hagiography 3%, philosophy 3%, law 3%, medicine 2%.

A hand-written regex taxonomy over `msItem` titles was tried first and rejected: it reached only
61% and silently dropped Boethius, Terence, *Le Roman de la Rose* and Petrarch. Use the
authority file. Do not invent a taxonomy the cataloguers did not.

## What is guessed, and what is not

**Guessable axes — only these three:**

1. **Position in time** (both games)
2. **Region of origin** — five-way choice (Look Closer)
3. **Subject** — what kind of book it is (Look Closer)

**Reveal-only, never a question:** former owners, institutional owners, acquisition, binding,
contents, bibliography, shelfmark. These are the reward for playing, not the test. This is a
deliberate constraint: guessing which monastery owned a book is not a skill anyone can learn
from looking at it, so asking would be noise dressed as difficulty.

## Game 1 — Ante Quem

**Mechanic: timeline insertion.** A card appears; place it among the cards already on the board.

Round 1 has two slots, which is exactly "older or newer?". Round 11 has twelve. The difficulty
ramps by itself with no tuning — random-guess success falls from 50% to 8.3% — and the game
starts at the simplest binary choice a person can be asked to make.

**Deck rule that keeps every answer defensible:** a card may only be dealt if its date range is
disjoint from the ranges of the two cards that would sit either side of it in the true order.
Redraw otherwise. 82% of pairs are already disjoint, so there is ample headroom.

**Difficulty ladder:** for round *k*, require the gap to the nearest neighbour on the board to be
at least G(k), decaying geometrically from 300 years to a 25-year floor:

    G(k) = max(25, round(300 * 0.75 ** (k - 1)))
    # 300, 225, 169, 127, 95, 71, 53, 40, 30, 25, 25, ...

Measured availability supports every rung: 25% of pairs are ≥300y apart, 56% ≥100y, 77% ≥25y.
In endless mode G stays at the 25-year floor once reached.

**Deck stratification:** draw across century buckets rather than uniformly, or 41% of every deck
is 15th-century and the board jams.

### The wager

This is what stops a binary choice from being shallow.

Each card opens showing about 14% of the page. You may expand — three times, to roughly 30%,
58%, then the full page — and each expansion cuts the round's score:

| Expansions used | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 0 | ×1.00 |
| 1 | ×0.70 |
| 2 | ×0.45 |
| 3 | ×0.25 |

Every round is now a decision under uncertainty — *do I trust my eye?* — layered on a judgment
simple enough to make in two seconds. Base 1,000 points per round.

### Modes

- **Daily** — 10 cards, deck seeded from the date so everyone plays the same run, 3 lives.
- **Endless** — until you are wrong.
- **Hard** — tightest crop only, no expansion.

### Share grid

Spoiler-free, one row per card, square = accuracy, arrow = which way you were wrong.

    Ante Quem #142   8/10
    🟩🟩🟨🟩🟥🟩🟩🟩🟨🟩

## Game 2 — Look Closer

No comparison. One manuscript, and the exploration *is* the gameplay.

The page opens almost entirely masked. You have a budget of five reveals: click anywhere to
uncover a patch at full zoom. **Where you choose to look is the skill being tested** — an
experienced eye goes to the script and the initials; a novice spends reveals on the picture.

Then commit to a reading:

1. **Century** — slider
2. **Region** — five-way choice
3. **Subject** — what kind of book, from the authority vocabulary

Score is accuracy weighted by reveals unspent. A confident reading from two patches beats a
correct one from five.

This works because the page genuinely tells you: liturgy is large script with rubrics and
sometimes notation; scholastic theology is glossed and double-columned; classical texts sit in
humanist single columns with wide margins; documents are in charter hands.

## Shared — the Shelf

Everything you meet in either game accumulates. The Shelf is the connective tissue and the
exploration surface: a coverage map of what you have encountered by century, region and
collection, with the gaps visible. *You have seen 47 of 2,260. You have nothing from Ireland.*

Every entry links to its Bodleian record. This is where the project's public-humanities purpose
actually lands — not in the score, in the accumulated traffic to the catalogue.

## The reveal

Fires after every round of either game. Two registers.

**What the catalogue says** — shelfmark; the date in the cataloguer's own words ("14th century,
first quarter"); origin; contents; language; material; decoration; the chain of former owners;
how it reached the Bodleian; bibliography. Then two links out: the catalogue record at
`medieval.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/catalog/manuscript_NNNN`, and the full image set on Digital Bodleian.

**What the corpus says** — computed at build time, and the part no catalogue page gives you:

- "Only 47 other Oxford manuscripts were made in Paris in this quarter-century."
- "Francis Douce owned 281 of the manuscripts in this game."
- "Mainz Charterhouse: 143 books, dispersed, reassembled here."

**And the teaching, where it exists** — 828 playable records carry a palaeographer's description
of the hand. Shown next to the crop you were just staring at, these name the exact letterforms
that date the page:

> "Fols. 1–16 in anglicana with secretary **a**; fols 17 to end mixed anglicana and secretary,
> with secretary **a** but **d, g, r,** and **s** of both kinds."

> "An English Vernacular Minuscule using insular forms. **a**: insular; **ð**: long ascender
> curving to the right and a round bowl; **ƿ**: with long tapered descender. s. xii"

That is the difference between "wrong, it was 1150" and being taught. The content is already
written, by Bodleian staff.

**Then the look-alike strip** — six other manuscripts from the same date and place. *This is
what Paris, 1300–1325 looks like.* Fires for 2,056 of 2,260.

## Architecture

A static site. No server, no database, no runtime cost, nothing that can go down unattended.

    build.py  →  data/puzzles.json      (~2,260 records)
                 data/context.json      (corpus aggregates for the reveal)
                 data/lookalikes.json   (date+place buckets)
              →  static site, vanilla JS
              →  GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages

`build.py` reads the tarball and emits committed JSON, so the site is reproducible from this
repo alone and re-runs only when the Bodleian updates.

### IIIF strategy — the critical constraint

**Bodleian's IIIF server is intermittently unreliable.** Measured on 2026-09-09: roughly 1 in 5
requests hung for 40s or returned 504, and it struck sizes the server advertises as
pre-rendered (`full/341,`) as readily as novel region crops. Tile requests flaked at a similar
rate — 11 of 12 returned in 0.7–2.6s, one timed out.

Therefore, and this is not optional:

1. **One request per manuscript.** Fetch `full/682,` (≈288 KB, ~1.2s) and nothing else.
2. **All cropping happens client-side** on a canvas. Every expansion step and every Look Closer
   reveal is a local operation — instant, and correct even if the network has gone.
3. **Prefetch two to three rounds ahead.** A slow response must never block the player.
4. **Retry with backoff, and skip the card** rather than stalling if it will not come.
5. Stick to round widths (400/512/600/800/1024). Avoid 341 and 420, both of which reliably
   timed out on the test image.

If the flakiness persists, fall back to caching the ~2,260 page images at build time onto
Cloudflare R2. At 682px that is roughly 650 MB; at 341px, ~160 MB.

### puzzles.json — one record

Illustrative shape, using MSS. Douce 211-12 for its real values where they exist.

    {
      "id": "manuscript_4472",
      "shelfmark": "MSS. Douce 211-12",
      "catalogue": "https://medieval.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/catalog/manuscript_4472",
      "iiif": "https://iiif.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/iiif/image/a6c8a0d0-...",
      "digitalBodleian": "https://digital.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/objects/ca766e0e-...",
      "attribution": "Photo: © Bodleian Libraries... CC BY-NC 4.0",
      "date": { "notBefore": 1300, "notAfter": 1325, "display": "14th century, first quarter" },
      "place": { "key": "place_7008038", "display": "Paris, France", "region": "France" },
      "subjects": ["bible"],
      "focus": { "cx": 0.44, "cy": 0.34 },
      "language": "fro", "material": "perg",
      "hand": "Written in a good gothic textualis...",
      "layout": "2 columns, 40 lines",
      "decoration": ["Fine miniatures.", "Borders."],
      "owners": [{ "name": "Francis Douce, 1757–1834", "role": "fmo" }],
      "acquisition": "Bequeathed to the Bodleian in 1834",
      "lookalikes": ["manuscript_10066", "..."]
    }

`focus` is chosen at build time: sample several candidate crops per page and keep the one with
the highest ink/colour variance, so no puzzle opens on blank parchment.

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| IIIF server flakiness (measured ~20%) | One fetch per manuscript, client-side crops, prefetch, retry, R2 fallback |
| 41% of the set is 15th-century | Stratified deck sampling across century buckets |
| 18% of pairs have overlapping ranges | Disjointness rule at deal time; redraw |
| Catalogue text licence unstated | Confirm with the Bodleian before public launch |
| Crop lands on blank parchment | Build-time variance selection for `focus` |
| Look Closer's subject axis feels arbitrary | Vocabulary is the cataloguers', not ours; show the term they assigned |

## Out of scope for v1

Three further surfaces read the same extraction and are deliberately deferred:
**Collector's Cabinet** (Canonici's 1,586 books against Laud's 384 — collecting taste as a
chart), the **origin atlas**, and the **survival curve** (Augustine 737 copies, Bede 210).
Building the spine first costs nothing toward any of them.

## Testing

- `build.py` is deterministic: same tarball in, byte-identical JSON out. Assert it.
- Every dealt card satisfies the disjointness rule — property test over 10,000 random deals.
- Every `catalogue` URL resolves (sample 50 per build).
- Daily seed produces the same deck for the same date across runs and browsers.
- Scoring is a pure function; unit-test the multiplier table and the range-aware credit.
- No puzzle has an empty `focus`, absent `iiif`, or a date range wider than 200 years.

## Phasing

Implement in two milestones. Milestone 1 is independently shippable and proves the spine.

**Milestone 1 (~3 days)** — `build.py`, the three data files, Ante Quem with daily and endless,
the reveal panel, the share grid. This is a complete, publishable game on its own.

**Milestone 2 (~3 days)** — Look Closer, the Shelf, hard mode, the look-alike strip.

Do not start Milestone 2 until Milestone 1 is deployed and playable. If the IIIF fallback to R2
proves necessary, it lands between the two.
